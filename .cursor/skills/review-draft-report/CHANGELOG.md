# Review draft report — feedback log

How this workflow evolved from real use. Writing is a hobby; the review should help me think, not stress me out.

---

## v3 (current) — Aug 2026

v2 was shorter and clearer, but it still felt like a to-do list. Marking items done, counting what was open, going through suggestions one by one: that framed writing as fixing problems, not reflecting on a draft.

v3 dropped all of that. Feedback is read-only prose in six themes (title, clarity, logic, voice, emotional). Each section says what's working, what might deserve a second look, and a short stage line like "Strong" or "Good enough." I read it, think about it, and change the text only if I want to. A new review round updates the feedback; there is no progress bar.

**Feedback that drove this:**

> "I don't like the idea of 'tasks' or 'issues' to be addressed. I want to remove completely the idea of 'progress' on fixing a draft report."

> "The feedbacks will serve as points to reflection, so I can decide what I will keep or change."

> "I appreciate title suggestions that make things clearer and stronger, but not ones that change my ideas or suppress my tone."

After the first test run, the format was tightened: split each card into "what's good" and "what needs attention," add a simple stage prefix at the top, and skip nitpicks entirely.

> "If something is nitpick or optional, remove. I don't want to overload my mind on minor details that won't have impact."

---

## v2 — Aug 2026

The first version overwhelmed me. Thirty-six items on one post. Generic advice without examples. Too many statuses; I couldn't tell what I had actually fixed. Writing should be relaxing; this made me stressed.

v2 aimed for short rounds, major issues only, and concrete examples (a real rewrite, not "be clearer"). Fewer statuses, simpler screen, re-reviews that update existing items instead of starting over. I also said clearly: lighter does not mean ignoring clarity, coherence, or organization.

**Feedback that drove this:**

> "The reports are heavy and overwhelmed me… writing should be a relax moment for me."

> "Each item had a suggestion to fix, but they feel generic. A concrete example would be good."

> "The bloat of status is a mess. I get confused on what I did wrong or right."

> "Lighter the review and simplify the process, but it doesn't mean that major issues should be ignored."

v2 helped a lot, but the checklist feeling remained. That led to v3.

---

## v1 — Jul 2026

The original idea was thorough: cover every angle (clarity, fairness, structure, emotion, and more) in one big report. In practice it generated too much, too fast, for a personal blog that a few friends read.

**Feedback that drove the change:**

> "My first try was a list of 36 items. Honestly, it was stressful."

> "I think we jumped to a complex system without testing simplicity."

---

## What I want (unchanged)

- Reviews for a hobby blog, not professional publishing.
- Substance over polish (grammar stays in `revise-draft-inline`).
- Honest feedback I can agree or disagree with.
- No nitpicks, no pretending thousands of people will read it.
- Preserve my voice, especially in title suggestions.
